Before We Start

In 60 Seconds

The essentials

  • Wiom's customer system is built as 8 Operating Systems — each owns one slice of the customer journey and nothing else
  • Every OS follows 4 Constitutional rules (C1–C4) that no OS can override — these are the non-negotiable promises to the customer
  • Each OS is a state machine driven by events — a customer is always in exactly one state per OS, and that state only changes when a real-world event triggers a transition
  • The OSes talk to each other through defined contracts — no manual coordination, no human in the loop for routine decisions
  • Build starts June 11. This week exists to catch surprises before they become expensive
Part 1

The Tiffin Service Analogy

Imagine a neighbourhood tiffin service. A family signs up for daily tiffin delivery. They pay weekly — no contract, stop anytime. The tiffin service makes the food, delivers it, and handles complaints.

At 10 families, the owner can manage everything in his head. He knows who likes less spice, whose dabba was late yesterday, and who hasn't paid this week. At 1,000 families, it's chaos. Orders get mixed up. Complaints get lost. One person's fix breaks another person's delivery.

So the tiffin service splits into departments. An order management department. A kitchen department. A delivery department. A complaints department. A billing department. Each department owns one thing and does it well. They don't reach into each other's domain. They communicate through defined handoffs. And all departments follow four company-wide rules that no department can override.

🍱 The analogy

That's what Customer OS is. Each OS is a department. Each department owns one truth — and only that truth. And all departments follow four rules that cannot be bypassed by any individual team.

Part 2

The 4 Rules Nobody Can Break

These are the Constitutional rules. Every OS, every feature, every agent action, and every automated message must comply with them. They cannot be overridden by any team or any system — only by the Founder.

C1 Canonical Truth
🍱 The tiffin version

If the tiffin service's order book says "Ravi's delivery is confirmed for Thursday," then the kitchen whiteboard, the delivery boy's list, and the family's receipt must all say the same thing. If one says Thursday and another says Friday, someone is lying. It doesn't matter who made the error — it's a system failure, not a display bug.

At Wiom

If the customer app says "your installation is confirmed for Thursday," the CSP app, support dashboard, and ops tools must all say the same thing. When a customer calls support and the agent looks at a different system, both must show the same state. Divergence is a system failure — not an acceptable inconsistency to be sorted out manually.

What this prevents

An agent telling a customer "your issue is resolved" when the system still shows it open. Two support agents giving different answers about the same booking because they're looking at different tools.

C2 System-Owned Failure
🍱 The tiffin version

If the delivery boy dropped the tiffin and the food spilled, the tiffin service cannot say "the family should have met the boy at the door" or "please submit a complaint form and we'll review in 3 days." The service caused it. The service fixes it. The service compensates. The family doesn't need to prove anything.

At Wiom

If the internet went down because of Wiom's infrastructure, Recovery OS must treat it as Wiom's obligation — automatically, without the customer needing to call or complain. The customer never pays — in time, money, or effort — for our mistakes. Attribution is honest. When the fault is ours, we own it.

What this prevents

Sending a recharge reminder to a customer whose internet is currently down because of a Wiom-side fault. Asking a customer to "raise a ticket" for a problem the system already knows about and caused.

C3 Restorative Recovery
🍱 The tiffin version

If the tiffin service owes a family a refund for spoiled food, the amount shouldn't depend on which person answers the phone, how loudly the family complains, or whether the manager is around. The rules say: 1 spoiled meal = 1 free meal. Same for every family. No "let me check with my manager." No "we can offer you 50 paise."

At Wiom

Recovery OS compensation follows locked formulas. 2-hour outage = 2 hours added to your plan. Same calculation for every customer. No agent discretion. No "save budget this month." The compensation must be experienceable as real restoration — not a token.

What this prevents

One agent giving a ₹50 coupon and another giving nothing for the identical outage on the same day. Compensation amounts that vary based on how politely the customer asked.

C4 Clarity of State
🍱 The tiffin version

If a family asks "is our tiffin coming today or not?" and the tiffin service says "we'll get back to you" — that's a C4 violation. The family should always know their current state and what comes next: the tiffin is on its way, it's delayed by 30 minutes, or it's cancelled today. No ambiguity.

At Wiom

Around 70% of early support chats were "kab lagega?" — customers didn't know their installation status after booking. C4 says the customer must always be able to answer three questions: what is happening right now, what happens next, and what should I do? "We are looking into it" fails C4. "Internet issue in your area — next update by 8 PM, no action needed from you" passes.

What this prevents

A customer calling support asking "kab lagega?" because nothing was communicated after the booking. A recovery message that says "we're working on it" without saying what that means or when to expect an update.

Part 3

Why Operating Systems, Not Just Features?

⚠️ The problem with features-first

Imagine the tiffin service adds a "reschedule delivery" feature. Works fine. Then a "track food quality" feature. Also fine. Then a "handle complaints" feature. Still fine — individually. But then: a family reschedules their delivery. During the gap, the quality system sees no delivery happened — so it opens a complaint automatically, which triggers compensation, which gives a free meal for a problem that didn't exist. Three features, each correct individually, producing wrong behaviour together. This happened in the CSP system. Features were added to handle situations as they arose. Together, they produced contradictions that were invisible until a customer hit them.

An OS is a governed boundary that makes four guarantees:

1. I own THIS truth — and only this truth. SQ Driver owns "is the internet working." Continuity OS owns "has the customer paid." Nobody else can overwrite my truth, and I don't reach into theirs.
2. I read from THESE sources — and only these sources. I consume events from specific sibling OSes through defined contracts. I don't guess. I don't read another OS's internal state directly.
3. I produce THESE outputs — and only these outputs. Other OSes can read what I emit, but I don't reach into their domain and make their decisions for them.
4. I follow THESE invariants — rules I can never break, no matter what. If Recovery OS's invariant says "ticket closure is never the same as recovery completion," then no agent action and no shortcut can violate that.
Part 4

5 Real Problems This Solves

These are problems that exist in the current system today. Every person in this room has seen at least two of them.

1
"Kab lagega?" — Nobody knows the status
Before

Customer books. System sends one notification. CSP may or may not respond. Customer has no idea what's happening. Calls support. Agent checks 3 tools — each shows a different status.

After

B2I OS has 16 defined states. The customer is in exactly one state at all times. Every transition requires a specific event. Customer app, CSP app, and support tools all read the same state. One question, one answer, always.

2
"Different agents, different answers"
Before

Internet goes down. Customer calls. One agent gives ₹50 credit. Another gives nothing. For the same outage. Depends on mood, tone, and whether the manager is around.

After

SQ Driver detects the outage and emits the state change. Recovery OS applies the compensation formula — same calculation for every customer. The event carries the truth; the rule produces the outcome. No agent makes the call.

3
"Pay us while we're broken"
Before

Customer has a service issue AND their recharge is expiring. Two separate teams handle them. The recharge team sends "please recharge" while the quality team hasn't fixed the internet.

After

SQ Driver emits degradation → Recovery opens a case → RS OS classifies the customer as Fragile → Continuity OS switches to "Recovery-first" posture automatically. No human coordination needed between teams.

4
"Silent churn nobody notices"
Before

Customer's recharge expires. Nobody notices for two weeks. By then they've gone back to the local cable operator. No record of what happened or why. They just disappeared.

After

Continuity OS tracks recharge posture. RS OS reclassifies the customer. If AT_RISK, Retention OS activates. If that fails, Exit OS takes over with full context. Every step is a named event. No customer disappears silently.

5
"Reconstruct what happened"
Before

Customer complains on social media. Team scrambles to understand what happened — checking 5 systems, Slack messages, call logs. Takes hours. Still incomplete.

After

Every state transition is a logged event with a timestamp and source OS. The full journey is traceable: BOOKING_CREATED → SLOT_CONFIRMED → INSTALL_COMPLETED → QUALITY_STATE_CHANGED → and so on. Complete audit trail in minutes.

Part 5

The 8 Operating Systems

Each OS owns exactly one slice of the customer journey. A customer moves through multiple OSes simultaneously as their life with Wiom progresses.

OS What it owns What it answers
B2I Booking-to-Install From paid booking to working internet. 16 states. Is the installation on track?
SQ Driver Service Quality Internet quality, measured continuously from router telemetry. 5 states. Is the internet actually working right now, and why?
Recovery OS Service failure handling from trigger to closure. Tracks Recovery Debt (accumulated damage). Is this customer's issue being handled properly? Has it compounded?
Continuity OS Payment posture and recharge handling. 8 postures. Should we ask this customer to recharge right now, or back off?
RS OS Relationship-State Overall relationship health. 5 states: Deep Active → Active → Stable → Fragile → AT_RISK. How is this customer's relationship with Wiom going?
Exit OS The customer is leaving. 2 state machines. Is this exit being handled with dignity? Has the device been collected?
CM OS Customer Memory Who this person is across their entire history with Wiom. The only OS at the person level — every other OS sees the connection. What does Wiom know about this customer across all their connections, past and present?
Retention OS Activated when a customer reaches AT_RISK. Is there anything we can do to keep this customer, and are we doing it?

Part 6

The Infrastructure Layer

Three registries sit beneath all 8 OSes and keep them connected. You don't need to know these in detail — but knowing they exist explains why things are done the way they are.

Event Semantic Registry (ESR)

The dictionary of every event any OS can emit. Each event has a defined name, a defined payload, and a guaranteed meaning. Nothing outside the ESR can be emitted.

System Parameter Registry (SPR)

Every tunable number in the system in one place — thresholds, window durations, compensation formula weights. Business adjustments happen here, not in the code.

Routing Registry

The map of who sends what to whom. For every event, it says: which OSes must consume it, which should, and which may. No signals get emitted into silence.

For CX / Execution / Comms / Business

What This Means for Your Daily Work

Today, when a customer calls about a problem, you have to figure out what's happening from scratch — check tickets, ask questions, sometimes guess. Customer OS changes this: every customer's state is always known. When a customer calls, you look up their state and the answer is already there. The system has been tracking what happened, when, and why.

CX / Support

You'll be checking OS state before asking the customer to re-explain. The state tells you what happened. Your job shifts from figuring out what happened to acting on what the system tells you. A repeat complaint is no longer routine — the system will flag it and treat it with higher urgency automatically.

Execution

Every handoff between teams has a defined state. A customer isn't "waiting" — they're in a specific state with a defined next step and a clock ticking. When a pickup SLA for a net box is about to breach, the system flags it — not because someone noticed, but because the state machine tracked it.

Comms

What you tell a customer must match what the system knows. "We are looking into it" is no longer acceptable — the system knows exactly what's happening, so the message must too. C4 means every message we send should let the customer answer: what is happening, what happens next, what should I do?

Business

The scenarios we'll walk through are situations your customers have been in. Your job is simple: tell us if what the system does in each scenario is right, different from what you see in the field, or missing something entirely.

We'll walk through real customer situations — payment failures, outages, installations that didn't go right, customers trying to leave. For each one: does this match what you see in the field? Is anything different? What's missing? You are pressure-testing the system before it gets built. That's the job.
