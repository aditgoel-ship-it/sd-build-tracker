§1 — State
🎓 ELI10: A traffic light has three states: RED, YELLOW, GREEN. While the light IS red, everyone behaves differently — cars stop, pedestrians cross. A state in our system is like that. "ACTIVATING" means: the customer paid, internet isn't on yet, everyone is acting to fix that. A state exists because people need to see it and act on it. Not because it technically happens.
What It Is

A state is a named condition of an entity during which the system may need to reason about it over time. While in a state, other systems know about it, behavior may differ, and the customer's situation is defined by it.

States are truth artifacts — they exist because the system needs to express a condition different from all others. They are NOT implementation artifacts — you don't create them because your code needs them.

The Test: If this transition fails halfway, does the system need to know where it is? If yes → state. If no → event.

The State Creation Rule — ALL THREE must be true
ConditionTestIf False
1. System can remain hereCan this condition persist — normally or on failure?If always atomic → event, not state
2. Another actor reasons about itDoes any other OS, surface, or human need to act differently?If only originating system cares → implementation detail
3. Behavior changesDoes behavior, communication, ownership, or measurement differ?If identical to adjacent state → redundant, collapse

Worked Example: Customer Reschedules Installation Slot

This is the example that prompted this document. Should RESCHEDULE_PENDING be a state?

Version A — Instant slot swap: Customer picks new slot from dropdown. System confirms instantly. One transaction. Result: NOT a state — it's an event. CONFIRMED → RESCHEDULE_REQUESTED event → CONFIRMED(new).

Version B — CSP-approval reschedule: Customer requests new date. CSP must check availability and approve. Takes minutes to hours. Result: YES, a state — CONFIRMED → RESCHEDULE_PENDING → CONFIRMED(new). With watchdog, hold logic, and customer projection.

The same concept — two answers. The question is never "is rescheduling transitory?" The question is: in OUR system, does it involve a step where the system can remain, where another actor reasons, and where behavior changes?

Two Traps:
1. "Transitory" trap — "This state only lasts seconds, so skip it." Wrong. ACTIVATING normally lasts seconds (LIVE path) but hours on PAUSED path. Duration is irrelevant. Apply the failure-halfway test.
2. State explosion trap — "Any condition that projects differently is a state." Wrong. Apply all three conditions of the State Creation Rule.
§2 — Event
🎓 ELI10: A doorbell rings. It happened. Everyone in the house hears it. You can't "un-ring" the doorbell. RECHARGE_CONFIRMED rings. Continuity starts the watchdog. CAEO checks the connection. Projection tells the customer "Payment received ✓." An event is something that happened. You can't undo it. You can only react to it.

§5 — Parameter
🎓 ELI10: The TV has a volume knob. Turn it up — louder. Turn it down — quieter. But the TV is still playing the same show. A parameter is the volume knob. "CSP grace window = 2 hours." Turn it to 3 hours — same system, just longer window. But "let's remove the grace window entirely" — that's not turning a knob. That's changing the channel. That's a doctrine change. Parameters adjust the intensity. Doctrine chooses the channel.

What is Customer OS?
Customer OS is the set of 10 systems that govern everything Wiom does on the customer side — from the moment someone books internet until they leave. It is not an app or a database. It is the logic layer that decides what happens, when, and why.
🎯 The Core Problem This Solves

Without Customer OS: every team makes ad-hoc decisions, truth lives in spreadsheets and chat, who's responsible for failures is guesswork, and no one knows what "recovery" actually means.

With Customer OS: every state, every event, every action is tracked and governed. The system produces structured truth. Every team reads that truth and acts on it.

The base unit is a single internet connection (called a service_instance_id internally) — not the customer themselves. One installation = one service instance. This is intentional.

The 4 Constitutional Rules
Every OS must follow all four. These rules can never be broken, even for business convenience.
C1
One Version of Truth
One consistent version of truth across every surface — app, CSP app, support tool, ops dashboard. Contradictions are flagged immediately. No team or surface gets to maintain its own version of the truth.
C2
System-Owned Failure
When Wiom or its partners fail, the customer bears zero consequences. CSP no-show = Wiom's problem. RDNI (paid but no internet) = Wiom's problem. The customer is never penalised for system failure.
C3
Restorative Recovery
Recovery must be proportional, substantive, and experienced by the customer as meaningful. A ₹10 coupon for a 3-day outage is not recovery. Extensions are proportional by construction. Coupons carry proportionality risk.
C4
Clarity of State
~70% of customer support chat today is "kab lagega?" — this is a C4 violation at scale. The customer must always know: what is happening, what is next, what they need to do. Silence is not a state.

🏠 What B2I OS is, in plain English

Think of B2I like the school admissions process. A student (customer) applies and pays the fee. Now the school (Wiom) has to verify their details, find a teacher (CSP/partner), schedule an orientation (installation), and confirm it worked.

The OS governs every step from payment to the moment internet is working — or to the moment it's clear it won't work. It tracks exactly what happened and why, so nothing ever falls into a black hole.

The key metric is NOT "how many installs did we do." It's "do we always know why every failed install failed, and was it our fault or theirs?"

🔑 5 Rules That Make B2I Different
Rule 1 — No auto-confirmation. A customer's silence is NEVER treated as a "yes" to an install date. The system escalates (S6), then proposes closure (S7), then drops with refund (S13) if still no response. This replaced a system that auto-confirmed silently.
Rule 2 — Who will be home? You can't send a technician if no one is home. The system must capture this before dispatch (install-contact field). If not resolved → escalation → eventual drop.
Rule 3 — S10 is new and critical. Previously, the system had no state for "technician is actively doing the install." This caused massive attribution failures. Now we know if a CSP claims the customer was absent — was a tech actually dispatched and tracked?
Rule 4 — Every failed install has a reason. Three attribution buckets: customer-truth / CSP-execution / system. "Unknown" as a lazy default is not allowed. If both parties give conflicting stories → customer truth is default pending investigation.
Rule 5 — S13 and S15 are separate. S13 records the fact (booking ended + why). S15 is the command to Exit OS (handle refund). Not every S13 needs an S15 — but the fact must precede the command.
🆕 New in v3.2

Four-tier entity hierarchy: B2I now explicitly tracks four linked entities for every booking: customer_id (the person, from CM OS) → service_instance_id (this subscription) → connection_id (the physical line/topology) → netbox_id (the hardware device). This four-tier structure is the canonical entity model across all Customer OSes. B2I is the first OS to create these relationships at booking time.

Fast-track re-entry: When CM OS signals that a returning customer's hardware is still in the network (custody_state = HARDWARE_PENDING_PICKUP), B2I skips new hardware provisioning. The returning customer is re-connected to their existing netbox rather than being treated as a fresh installation. Faster activation, fewer hardware costs.

Service transfer management: B2I OS now coordinates the service transfer flow (customer relocating to a new address). It emits TRANSFER_IN_PROGRESS to Continuity OS (which enters Transfer-Suspended posture) and TRANSFER_COMPLETED (A17) or TRANSFER_CANCELLED (A18) to Exit OS when the transfer resolves. This was previously unspecified — it is now a formal B2I responsibility.

🆕 v3.2 — Entity Hierarchy, Fast-Track Re-entry & Transfer Management
Four-tier entity hierarchy (canonical): customer_id (CM OS — person, durable) → service_instance_id (B2I — this subscription) → connection_id (physical line/topology) → netbox_id (hardware device). B2I is the OS that creates the service_instance_id and binds it to the other three. All downstream OSes reference service_instance_id as the primary partition key for all events.
Fast-track re-entry: If CM OS emits CUSTOMER_REENTRY_INITIATED with custody_state = HARDWARE_PENDING_PICKUP, B2I skips hardware provisioning and re-binds the customer to their existing netbox_id. The four-tier hierarchy is restored without a new hardware loop. Key upstream dependency: CM OS must surface Exit OS custody_state in the re-entry signal (AMD-FS-10).

⛔ What B2I OS never does
❌ Does not monitor quality after installation. The moment Install Completed fires, B2I's job ends. SQ Driver takes over from there.
❌ Does not auto-confirm a slot if the customer doesn't respond. Customer silence triggers escalation, then Closure Proposed, then a refund — but never a silent "yes." Auto-confirmation is never allowed.
❌ Does not govern recharge, retention, or ongoing service. Its scope is strictly booking-to-install. What happens after the router lights up belongs to other OSes.
❌ Does not allow "unknown" as a lazy attribution bucket. Every failed install has a structured reason: customer-truth, CSP-execution, or system. "Unknown" is only permitted after genuine investigation, never as a default.
❌ Does not let a booking drift. Every state has a timeout and escalation rule. The system always acts. Limbo is not a state.

📈 Recovery Debt

Recovery Debt is a running tally of accumulated damage — it doesn't go away when a case closes. It tracks the cumulative harm caused by quality failures over a 30-day rolling window.

Weights: Unusable event = 3× · Intermittent = 2× · Degraded = 1×

This prevents 3 minor degraded events from "outweighing" one catastrophic 3-day outage in the debt score.

Key rule: Cases can close while Recovery Debt remains active. These are different layers. Debt doesn't block closure — it's a separate long-term risk signal.

🩺 What At-Risk Resolution OS is, in plain English

Think of At-Risk Resolution OS like a doctor treating an illness. Their job is to fix the problem — the deterioration in the customer's service experience. Whether the patient decides to stay or leave afterwards is not the doctor's outcome metric.

When RS OS classifies a customer as AT_RISK (Medium+), this OS opens a bounded, time-limited case. It has exactly 8 tools — no more, no less. It has a hard 90-day cap. It never tells the customer they are AT_RISK. It never offers "stay and get X" incentives.

A customer who exits with their deterioration fully resolved is a success for this OS. The goal is never to keep someone — it is to ensure that if they leave, it is not because of something Wiom failed to fix.

Formerly called Retention OS. The name changed deliberately — "retention" imports the subscription-company mental model. This OS resolves problems. It is not a save desk.

📍 What Triggers This OS
Sole automatic trigger: RS OS emits Medium+ AT_RISK classification. That's it. Does NOT trigger on: Fragile alone, non-recharge alone, low usage, app inactivity, "likely churn," or any marketing team request.

Hour 24: Outage exceeds CP-REC-14 threshold. Interim compensation permitted while ongoing. RECOVERY_COMPENSATION_DETERMINED emitted: compensation_stage=interim, incident_status=ongoing, compensation_type=extension. Seema gets acknowledgment credit while outage is still happening.
4
DEPENDENT weighting (CP-REC-10 = 2×): Seema's recovery effort is weighted more heavily. More frequent updates. Escalation triggers faster. Compensation formula: outage_duration × severity × DEPENDENT_weight. Constitution Steward audits proportionality.
5
Hour 72: Fiber fixed. SQ Driver emits QUALITY_STATE_CHANGED: STATE=Healthy. Technical_state → restored. RESTORATION_WINDOW_ACTIVE starts (requires T time + N healthy intervals before closing). Case moves to verification_pending.
6
3-of-4 closure check: Operational ✅ (fiber physically repaired) · Technical ✅ (telemetry Healthy) · Workflow ✅ (restoration communication sent) · Experiential: pending. System waits CP-REC-12 days. No contradiction from Seema + telemetry stable = sufficient. Case closes. Final compensation determined (proportional to 72h, DEPENDENT weighted).
Key: INV-REC-14 (extensions before coupons) and the proportionality doctrine mean Seema's 72-hour outage gets 72+ hours of service credit — not a ₹50 coupon. The system distinguishes interim (ongoing acknowledgment) from final (proportional restoration). CP-REC-14 gates the interim mechanism.
Recovery OS · Scenario 9
Ownership Failure — Caught Before It Happens

Arun files a complaint about intermittent internet. A reactive recovery case is opened. Owner assigned: Support Agent Preeti. Preeti sends the acknowledgment. Then Preeti goes on leave and forgets to transfer the case.

36 hours later: Arun has not received any update. Case status: in_progress. Preeti's status: on leave. No escalation has happened.

What does Recovery OS do? Can a case ever have no owner? What prevents Arun from falling into the "silent abandonment" failure mode?
1
INV-REC-04: Every open case must have a non-null owner_id at all times. The moment Preeti's status changes (leave flag in support system), the case must be transferred. Ownership gap = architectural failure — not a soft warning.
2
Even without a system flag: CP-REC-03 (acknowledged → in_progress timeout) fires. If no workflow progress within the governed window, auto-escalation triggers. Case moves to escalation_state = auto_escalated.
3
CP-REC-05 (update cadence) also fires. Arun has not received an update in 36 hours. RECOVERY_COMMUNICATION_OBLIGATION emitted for type=update. If the Communications layer doesn't fire this (because no owner is managing the case) → that's itself a failure signal that triggers escalation.
4
Manager escalation kicks in. A supervisor-level owner is assigned. Arun gets: "Hi Arun, we're aware of your internet issue and [Name] is now managing your case. Expected resolution update by 5 PM today." Not "escalated to senior team." Not "we're looking into it."
5
The old pattern (without Recovery OS): Arun's ticket sits for 3-4 days, he never hears back, he files another complaint, reopen case now has 10.5pp lower R30. He quietly churns at renewal. With Recovery OS: the abandonment is structurally prevented by the combination of ownership invariant + timeout escalation + communication obligation.
Key: The Four Failure Families in Recovery OS rank ownership #1 — above communication, above compensation. "No one owns this" destroys meta-trust: the customer stops believing the system can recover at all. The architectural controls (non-null owner + timeout escalation) exist precisely because human handoffs fail.
