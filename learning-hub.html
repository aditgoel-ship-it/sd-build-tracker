What is Customer OS?
Customer OS is the system of 10 Operating Systems that govern everything Wiom does on the customer side — from the moment someone books internet until they leave. It is not an app or a database. It is the logic layer that decides what happens, when, and why.
🎯 The Core Problem This Solves

Without Customer OS: every team makes ad-hoc decisions, truth lives in spreadsheets and chat, failure attribution is guesswork, and no one knows what "recovery" actually means.

With Customer OS: every state, every event, every attribution is governed. The system produces structured truth. Downstream teams consume that truth and act on it.

The canonical unit is always service_instance_id — not customer_id. One installation = one service instance. This is intentional.

The 4 Constitutional Rules
Every OS must satisfy all four. These can never be violated, even for business convenience.
C1
Canonical Customer Truth
One authoritative version of truth across every surface — app, CSP app, support tool, ops dashboard. Divergence is flagged, not tolerated. No surface gets its own version of the truth.
C2
System-Owned Failure
When Wiom or its partners fail, the customer bears zero consequences. CSP no-show = Wiom's problem. RDNI (paid but no internet) = Wiom's problem. The customer is never penalised for system failure.
C3
Restorative Recovery
Recovery must be proportional, substantive, and experienced by the customer as meaningful. A ₹10 coupon for a 3-day outage is not recovery. Extensions are proportional by construction. Coupons carry proportionality risk.
C4
Clarity of State
~70% of customer support chat today is "kab lagega?" — this is a C4 violation at scale. The customer must always know: what is happening, what is next, what they need to do. Silence is not a state.

📖 "B2I OS captures truth. It does not manufacture conversions."
🏠 What B2I OS is, in plain English

Think of B2I like the school admissions process. A student (customer) applies and pays the fee. Now the school (Wiom) has to verify their details, find a teacher (CSP/partner), schedule an orientation (installation), and confirm it worked.

The OS governs every step from payment to the moment internet is working — or to the moment it's clear it won't work. It tracks exactly what happened and why, so nothing ever falls into a black hole.

The key metric is NOT "how many installs did we do." It's "do we always know why every failed install failed, and was it our fault or theirs?"

🔑 5 Rules That Make B2I Different
Rule 1 — No auto-confirmation. A customer's silence is NEVER treated as a "yes" to an install date. The system escalates (S6), then proposes closure (S7), then drops with refund (S13) if still no response. This replaced a system that auto-confirmed silently.
Rule 2 — Who will be home? You can't send a technician if no one is home. The system must capture this before dispatch (install-contact field). If not resolved → escalation → eventual drop.
Rule 3 — S10 is new and critical. Previously, the system had no state for "technician is actively doing the install." This caused massive attribution failures. Now we know if a CSP claims the customer was absent — was a tech actually dispatched and tracked?
Rule 4 — Every failed install has a reason. Three attribution buckets: customer-truth / CSP-execution / system. "Unknown" as a lazy bucket is constitutionally prohibited. If both parties give conflicting stories → customer truth is default pending investigation.
Rule 5 — S13 and S15 are separate. S13 records the fact (booking ended + why). S15 is the command to Exit OS (handle refund). Not every S13 needs an S15 — but the fact must precede the command.
📖 "B2I OS captures truth. It does not manufacture conversions."
Design priority order: (1) Improve customer truth → (2) Reduce CSP-side absorption → (3) Improve install conversion. Integrity governs. Conversion validates. If conversion improves but integrity is weak, the OS is manufacturing conversions.

📡 "SQ Driver emits truth. It does not fix internet."
🩺 What SQ Driver is, in plain English

Think of SQ Driver as a doctor who only does diagnosis — never prescribes medicine. It constantly monitors every customer's internet (through router telemetry) and classifies what it observes: is the internet healthy, slow, cutting in and out, or just not working?

It then tells other systems what it found. Recovery OS hears "this customer has been without internet for 6 hours" and opens a recovery case. Recharge OS hears "this customer's internet is broken" and adjusts its nudge posture. SQ Driver just reports. It never acts.

The most important thing SQ Driver proved with data: Unknown (we can't tell what's wrong) is worse than Unusable (we know it's broken). Customers you can't see are more likely to churn than customers you know have bad internet.

Why Unknown is the worst: Unknown (74.1% R30) is worse than Unusable (82.8% R30). If we know your internet is broken, we can act. If we can't see you at all — no telemetry, no signal — you drift away silently. Epistemic invisibility is more dangerous than known-bad service.
Why Intermittent > Degraded in severity: Stable slow internet (5 Mbps consistently) is more predictable than internet that cuts in and out. Instability destroys trust faster, makes recovery harder. Severity is downstream-consequence-weighted.
🔴 RDNI — The Highest Trust-Damage Failure

RDNI = Recharge Done, No Internet. Customer pays → service does not resume. This is the worst failure because it combines demonstrated payment intent with zero service delivery — a C2 violation by construction.

Who owns what:

SQ Driver: detects it (STATE=Unusable, FAULT_DOMAIN=platform_system, ATTRIBUTION_DETAIL=service_not_resumed_after_payment). Full confidence.

Recharge OS / Platform: prevents it (H8 integration fix — eliminates this category entirely when done).

Recovery OS: handles the customer (opens proactive recovery case, highest urgency).

SQ Driver does NOT fix it. SQ Driver does NOT contact the customer. It reports truth.

⚠️ What SQ Driver cannot do (critical boundaries)
❌ Fix the internet — that's IE team
❌ Tell the customer about their outage — that's Recovery OS
❌ Suppress recharge nudges — that's Recharge OS deciding
❌ Give a quality score (0-100) — it emits decomposed facts, not synthesized scores
❌ Predict future states ("likely to degrade") — observed truth only, never prediction

🔧 "Recovery OS prevents and exposes Recovery Debt. It does not prove trust survived."
🏥 What Recovery OS is, in plain English

Think of Recovery OS as an ER doctor managing a patient case. When your internet goes down, Recovery OS opens a case, assigns a doctor (owner), makes sure the patient (you) is informed throughout, verifies the problem is truly fixed — not just "closed on paper" — and tracks if you keep coming back with the same problem.

What recovery looks like — the difference between good and bad:

✅ Good: "Internet issue in your area. Next update by 8 PM."
❌ Bad: "We are looking into it."

✅ Good: "Router issue detected. Restart not required from your side."
❌ Bad: "Please wait."

✅ Good: "We added 4 hours free service for the 2-hour outage yesterday."
❌ Bad: "Credit applied."

Recovery Doctrine: Recovery quality is evaluated by substantive restoration, not by operational completion, emotional persuasion, or performative actions (lots of messages that say nothing).

📐 Multi-Dimensional State Model (simplified)
Recovery does NOT have a single linear workflow. It tracks 4 independent dimensions simultaneously. Closure only happens when enough evidence is available across dimensions.

🔢 The 3-of-4 Evidence Rule for Closure
Ticket closed ≠ Recovery complete. This is INV-REC-01 and the most important thing Recovery OS does differently. Closure requires positive evidence from at least 3 of 4 layers:
1. Operational
✅ Work was completed (when physical work needed)
❌ Work not done or incomplete
2. Technical
✅ Telemetry confirms Healthy state
❌ Telemetry still shows degradation
3. Workflow
✅ Customer was informed of restoration
❌ Customer not informed
4. Experiential
✅ No recurrence + customer non-contradiction with healthy telemetry
❌ Active contradiction or unresolved recurrence
📈 Recovery Debt

Recovery Debt is longitudinal damage memory — it doesn't go away when a case closes. It tracks the cumulative harm caused by quality failures over a 30-day rolling window.

Weights: Unusable event = 3× · Intermittent = 2× · Degraded = 1×

This prevents 3 minor degraded events from "outweighing" one catastrophic 3-day outage in the debt score.

Key rule: Cases can close while Recovery Debt remains active. These are different layers. Debt doesn't block closure — it's a separate long-term risk signal.

🔁 Failed Recovery Re-entry
If the same root cause recurs on the same service_instance_id within 30 days (CP-REC-06) — that is NOT a fresh incident. It is damage-compounding failure. The system auto-detects this, emits FAILED_RECOVERY_REENTRY_DETECTED, increments Recovery Debt, and applies escalated posture + higher compensation logic.
🔧 "Recovery OS prevents and exposes Recovery Debt. It does not prove trust survived."
Category: Stock-Flow OS — governed stock is open recovery obligations (workflow-stock), not customer lifecycle states. Every open recovery case with incomplete closure obligations is a unit of governed stock.
Starts from: SQ emission (proactive) OR customer complaint routed through Issue Classification Layer (reactive) OR B2I BOOKING_DROPPED with CSP-execution/system attribution OR billing error.

⚠️ Why NPS Cannot Govern Recovery OS (Summary)
Full argument in Appendix B of the spec. Short version: NPS measures customer emotion, which can be improved through generous compensation, effusive apologies, and message volume — none of which require fixing the root cause. A system optimising for NPS will eventually produce high NPS and broken internet simultaneously. This is a documented failure mode. The 3-of-4 closure-integrity metric cannot be gamed through emotional manipulation — you can't fake telemetry confirmation.

4 dimensions progress independently — no global synchronisation. Closure requires positive evidence from ≥3 of 4 layers (3-of-4 rule). Recovery Debt is a separate aggregate layer that persists across case closures.

After 48h of silence (CP-06, default), booking moves from S5 → S6 (Escalation In Progress). System has not auto-confirmed the slot — INV-B2I-01 prevents this.
2
S6 runs the full escalation sequence: WhatsApp → IVR → Push. All channels personalized by channel_responsiveness profile. All attempts logged with outcome.
3
Escalation exhausted. Booking moves S6 → S7 (Closure Proposed). The system sends Priya an explicit message: "Confirm your slot, request a new date, or cancel. If we don't hear from you in [CP-07 days], your booking will be closed with a refund."
4
CP-07 window expires. Still no response. Booking moves S7 → S13 (Dropped with Attribution). Attribution bucket: customer-truth. Structured reason: "unresponsive to slot proposal — closure proposed, no response within window."
5
S13 → S15 (Exit OS Handoff). EXIT_HANDOFF command emitted with refund_trigger = true (customer never agreed to anything — C3 requires refund).
Key: This is NOT auto-cancellation. It's a governed process with multiple explicit chances. S7 (Closure Proposed) is the load-bearing state — without it, the system either auto-cancels (violates C2) or drifts in limbo indefinitely (violates C4 and INV-B2I-07).
B2I OS · Scenario 2
The Disputed No-Show

Rahul confirms his installation slot (S8). A technician is assigned (S9). On the day, the technician's app shows "install_attempt_started" and the booking moves to S10. An hour later, the CSP records via their app: "INSTALL_FAILED — customer not available at home." Booking drops to S13 with attribution: customer-truth.

Rahul immediately calls support: "I was home the entire day. The technician never came. He called me once and when I called back, nobody picked up."

What does B2I OS do now? Does the attribution change? Who is responsible? What happens to the booking?
1
Support uses their structured write-back authority (INV-B2I-15, Resolution #5) to record Rahul's account. This creates a truth divergence: CSP says "customer absent" vs customer says "technician never came."
2
TRUTH_DIVERGENCE_FLAGGED event emitted. The system captures: truth_source_1 (CSP, rank 5, "customer not available"), truth_source_2 (customer via support, rank 3, "technician no-show"), divergence_type: install_outcome.
3
Per INV-B2I-11: customer truth is the default pending resolution. Rahul's version is treated as authoritative for customer-facing purposes. The current attribution (customer-truth) is tentatively held while ops investigates.
4
Ops investigates within 24h SLA: checks technician's GPS log, call records, system event timestamps. If technician never reached the address → attribution changes to CSP-execution. BOOKING_DROPPED re-attributed. Quality OS notified.
5
If confirmed CSP fault: Rahul gets a new booking without penalty. No rescheduling count used. No strike against Rahul. Attribution = CSP-execution is a C2 issue — Wiom owns this failure.
Key insight: INV-B2I-11 is pro-customer by design. CSPs have structural incentive to misreport ("customer wasn't home" is easier than "I failed to execute"). Machine evidence (GPS, call logs) resolves when available. Customer truth is the default in the interim.
B2I OS · Scenario 3
The Successful Reroute

Anita books internet. CSP is assigned (S3 → S4). The CSP has 24 hours to propose slots (CP-05). They don't. The system waits. 30 hours later — still nothing from the CSP.

Meanwhile, Anita's app shows "Finding you an installation partner." She doesn't know what's happening.

What does B2I OS do? What state does Anita's booking enter? What does she see? How does this eventually resolve?
1
CP-05 timeout fires. CSP_SILENCE_TIMEOUT event emitted (compliance failure signal → Quality OS). Booking moves S4 → S11 (Booking Rerouted). reroute_count increments to 1.
2
C4 obligation: Anita MUST be notified of the reroute (INV from §5.5 visibility rules). She gets: "We're finding you a new installation partner — this won't affect your booking." She is not in the dark.
3
System assigns a new CSP. Booking moves S11 → S4 (Slot Proposal Pending again, new CSP). New CSP has CP-05 hours to propose slots.
4
New CSP proposes 3 slots. Anita confirms one explicitly via app action. SLOT_CONFIRMED emitted. Booking moves S4 → S5 → S8. The confirmation_mode field = "customer-confirmed" always.
5
Technician assigned (S9), install happens (S10), succeeds. INSTALL_COMPLETED emitted. Booking moves to S14. SQ Driver and Recharge OS begin governing Anita's service instance.
Key: Rerouting is Wiom's problem — Anita bears zero consequences. Her reroute_count doesn't count against her. The first CSP's silence is a compliance failure captured by Quality OS. Anita sees the app update in real time (C4). This scenario also shows that reroutes feed back to S4 — not to S1 or the start.

SQ Driver · Scenario 4
RDNI — Recharge Done, No Internet

Vijay's plan expires at midnight. At 12:01 AM he opens the app and recharges ₹399. The payment succeeds. He opens YouTube. It doesn't load. He waits 10 minutes. Still nothing. He checks WhatsApp. Nothing. He puts down his phone and goes to sleep.

At 6 AM he wakes up and tries again. Still no internet.

What is SQ Driver doing during this time? What does it emit, with what attribution? Who handles what?
1
SQ Driver detects: payment event received from Recharge OS (Vijay recharged) + no service resumption signal from netbox telemetry. Connection remains down.
2
QUALITY_STATE_CHANGED emitted: STATE=Unusable, FAULT_DOMAIN=platform_system, ATTRIBUTION_DETAIL=service_not_resumed_after_payment, ATTRIBUTION_CONFIDENCE=high, SCOPE=localized.
3
This is RDNI — the highest trust-damage preventable failure. It's a platform_system fault (provisioning/auth failed to resume service after payment). Customer demonstrated payment intent; system gave zero service. C2 violation by construction.
4
Recovery OS receives the QUALITY_STATE_CHANGED emission. It opens a proactive recovery case — highest urgency. Vijay gets an acknowledgment. The platform team is escalated immediately.
5
SQ Driver does NOT contact Vijay. Does NOT suppress the Recharge OS from acting. Does NOT issue a credit. It emits truth with high confidence attribution and lets the consumers act.
Key: The RDNI attribution split is critical. SQ owns detection + emission. Platform team owns prevention (H8 integration). Recovery OS owns the customer-facing response. No single OS owns all three. This is by design — one-directional flow.
SQ Driver · Scenario 5
The Invisible Problem

Meera has been calling support for 3 days saying her internet is not working. She's filed 3 tickets. But SQ Driver shows her netbox as Healthy — full telemetry, all green.

Your manager asks: "Does Meera have a quality problem or not? SQ says Healthy, customer says broken — whose truth do we use?"

How does SQ Driver handle this conflict? Does it override the telemetry? Does it emit Unknown? What happens next?
1
SQ Driver does NOT override telemetry with customer reports. Per INV-SQ-03: "When verified telemetry exists, it is canonical. Customer reports modify confidence but never override."
2
But 3 complaints in 3 days is a significant confidence signal. SQ Driver lowers ATTRIBUTION_CONFIDENCE and triggers QUALITY_INVESTIGATION_TRIGGERED — not because customer is right, but because there's a truth integrity gap to investigate.
3
Three possible realities: (a) Meera has a real problem telemetry isn't catching — device-level issue that netbox doesn't report. (b) Meera is misperceiving (phone OS issue, not internet). (c) Telemetry pipe is broken — data looks green but pipe is lying. SQ must find out which.
4
If (c): pipe-health check shows broken telemetry pipe → emit STATE=Unknown, FAULT_DOMAIN=unknown, confidence=low. QUALITY_INVESTIGATION_TRIGGERED to fix pipe.
5
Recovery OS handles Meera regardless — she has filed complaints, she gets a reactive recovery case. That recovery doesn't require SQ to have a definitive answer. Recovery can open on complaint alone.
Key: SQ Driver does not choose between "telemetry is right" or "customer is right." It investigates the discrepancy. The customer complaint modifies confidence and triggers investigation — it does not author state. This is Observed-Truth-Only in action.
SQ Driver · Scenario 6
The Area Outage

At 2 AM on a Tuesday, 14 netboxes in Sector 22, Noida start showing Unusable telemetry. The pattern develops over 20 minutes. By 2:20 AM, 18 netboxes are affected. All show the same dominant failure pattern in telemetry.

Zero customer complaints so far. Everyone is asleep.

What does SQ Driver do? Does it wait for customer complaints before acting? What events does it emit and to whom?
1
At CP-SQ-11 threshold (e.g., 10+ netboxes in zone non-Healthy), SQ Driver runs cluster-correlation analysis. 18 netboxes in the same zone, same fault pattern = area-wide signal.
2
CLUSTER_DEGRADATION_DETECTED emitted: zone_id = sector-22-noida, affected_service_instance_count = 18, dominant_fault_domain = local_distribution (or upstream_network based on evidence), confidence = high, detected_at = 2:20 AM.
3
Simultaneously, each affected service_instance gets QUALITY_STATE_CHANGED with STATE=Unusable, SCOPE=area-wide. They don't get individual isolated attributions — scope signals it's a cluster issue.
4
IE team receives structured intake: area-wide failure, 18 connections affected, fault domain, SLA = critical (area-wide outage → highest tier). Recovery OS receives proactive trigger for all 18 service instances — proactive cases open before anyone complains.
5
INV-SQ-17: this happens at 2 AM with full detection. Night-hour detection is not excluded. When customers wake up at 7 AM and try to connect, there's already a recovery case, an owner, and (if not fixed) an acknowledgment ready to send.
Key: SQ Driver does not wait for complaints. 79% of incidents today have no customer-facing communication (team data: 704K incidents, only 21% get Divi message). The entire point of proactive SQ detection is to flip this — system knows before customer complains.

Recovery OS · Scenario 7
The Compounding Failure

Sunil's internet goes Unusable due to a local_distribution issue. Recovery OS opens a case (proactive). Owner assigned. Acknowledgment sent. Technician visits. Telemetry confirms Healthy. 3-of-4 evidence passes. Case closed.

18 days later: same issue. Sunil's internet goes Unusable again. Same fault domain, same zone. SQ emits QUALITY_STATE_CHANGED again.

Is this a fresh incident or something else? How does Recovery OS treat it? What does the compensation look like?
1
Recovery OS runs cross-case correlation on every new case creation. Same root cause (local_distribution) + same service_instance_id + 18 days (within CP-REC-06 = 30-day window) = match detected.
2
FAILED_RECOVERY_REENTRY_DETECTED emitted: original_case_id + new_case_id + root_cause_match_confidence + recovery_debt_current. This is not a fresh incident — it is damage-compounding failure (INV-REC-12).
3
Recovery Debt increments: Unusable event = 3×. If Sunil already had a previous debt score, this adds to it significantly. Escalation posture elevates — manager_escalated, not just normal.
4
Compensation: NOT the same as a first incident. The recurrence multiplier applies. INV-REC-14: extensions before coupons. Sunil gets a proportional plan extension for the current outage duration. And likely a larger acknowledgment than a first-time case would get.
5
Root cause investigation is now mandatory — not just fix the symptom again. If local_distribution keeps failing for Sunil, the system tracks this and escalates the infrastructure repair urgency.
Key: The 30-day window (CP-REC-06 = CP-RS-10 = 30 days) is a cross-OS aligned parameter. Team data shows reopen cases have 10.5pp lower R30 and 2× exit rate. Failed Recovery Re-entry is not a cosmetic flag — it's a churn predictor treated as first-class signal.
Recovery OS · Scenario 8
The 3-Day Outage

A fiber cut causes a 72-hour outage for Seema. SQ Driver emits CLUSTER_DEGRADATION_DETECTED and individual QUALITY_STATE_CHANGED for Seema's service instance. Recovery OS opens a proactive case at hour 0 — before Seema even notices.

Seema is a DEPENDENT customer (her elderly mother uses the internet for medical video consultations).

Walk through the full Recovery OS response: communication obligations, escalation posture, compensation logic, closure evidence.
1
Hour 0: Case triggered proactively. Owner assigned (INV-REC-04 — never null). RECOVERY_COMMUNICATION_OBLIGATION emitted: type=acknowledgment, urgency=high. Seema gets: "We've detected an internet issue in your area. We're working on it. Next update by 2 PM." C4 obligation met.
2
Every CP-REC-05 hours: Update obligations emitted. Seema gets: "Still working on the fiber issue in your area. Expected resolution by tonight. We'll keep you updated." Not "Please wait." Not "We are looking into it."
3
Hour 24: Outage exceeds CP-REC-14 threshold. Interim compensation permitted while ongoing. RECOVERY_COMPENSATION_DETERMINED emitted: compensation_stage=interim, incident_status=ongoing, compensation_type=extension. Seema gets acknowledgment credit while outage is still happening.
4
DEPENDENT weighting (CP-REC-10 = 2×): Seema's recovery effort is weighted more heavily. More frequent updates. Escalation triggers faster. Compensation formula: outage_duration × severity × DEPENDENT_weight. Constitution Steward audits proportionality.
5
Hour 72: Fiber fixed. SQ Driver emits QUALITY_STATE_CHANGED: STATE=Healthy. Technical_state → restored. RESTORATION_WINDOW_ACTIVE starts (requires T time + N healthy intervals before closing). Case moves to verification_pending.
6
3-of-4 closure check: Operational ✅ (fiber physically repaired) · Technical ✅ (telemetry Healthy) · Workflow ✅ (restoration communication sent) · Experiential: pending. System waits CP-REC-12 days. No contradiction from Seema + telemetry stable = sufficient. Case closes. Final compensation determined (proportional to 72h, DEPENDENT weighted).
Key: INV-REC-14 (extensions before coupons) and the proportionality doctrine mean Seema's 72-hour outage gets 72+ hours of service credit — not a ₹50 coupon. The system distinguishes interim (ongoing acknowledgment) from final (proportional restoration). CP-REC-14 gates the interim mechanism.
Recovery OS · Scenario 9
Ownership Failure — Caught Before It Happens

Arun files a complaint about intermittent internet. A reactive recovery case is opened. Owner assigned: Support Agent Preeti. Preeti sends the acknowledgment. Then Preeti goes on leave and forgets to transfer the case.

36 hours later: Arun has not received any update. Case status: in_progress. Preeti's status: on leave. No escalation has happened.

What does Recovery OS do? Can a case ever have no owner? What prevents Arun from falling into the "silent abandonment" failure mode?
1
INV-REC-04: Every open case must have a non-null owner_id at all times. The moment Preeti's status changes (leave flag in support system), the case must be transferred. Ownership gap = architectural failure — not a soft warning.
2
Even without a system flag: CP-REC-03 (acknowledged → in_progress timeout) fires. If no workflow progress within the governed window, auto-escalation triggers. Case moves to escalation_state = auto_escalated.
3
CP-REC-05 (update cadence) also fires. Arun has not received an update in 36 hours. RECOVERY_COMMUNICATION_OBLIGATION emitted for type=update. If the Communications layer doesn't fire this (because no owner is managing the case) → that's itself a failure signal that triggers escalation.
4
Manager escalation kicks in. A supervisor-level owner is assigned. Arun gets: "Hi Arun, we're aware of your internet issue and [Name] is now managing your case. Expected resolution update by 5 PM today." Not "escalated to senior team." Not "we're looking into it."
5
The old pattern (without Recovery OS): Arun's ticket sits for 3-4 days, he never hears back, he files another complaint, reopen case now has 10.5pp lower R30. He quietly churns at renewal. With Recovery OS: the abandonment is structurally prevented by the combination of ownership invariant + timeout escalation + communication obligation.
Key: The Four Failure Families in Recovery OS rank ownership #1 — above communication, above compensation. "No one owns this" destroys meta-trust: the customer stops believing the system can recover at all. The architectural controls (non-null owner + timeout escalation) exist precisely because human handoffs fail.
