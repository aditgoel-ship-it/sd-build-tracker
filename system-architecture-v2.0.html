Why Read This
In 60 seconds: Wiom's customer system is governed by 8 locked Operating Systems, each owning one slice of truth about the customer's journey. These OSes communicate through events, follow 4 constitutional rules (C1–C4), and are constrained by 108 doctrines and 110+ invariants. The architecture replaces ad-hoc feature logic with bounded, auditable, constitutionally governed subsystems. It is designed for PayG — not subscription, not telco, not CRM.

After reading this: You should be able to look at any OS spec and know where it sits in the system, what truth it owns, what it reads, what it emits, and what it's constitutionally prohibited from doing.

What to do: Read this note fully (~25 min). Then read the spec of the OS closest to your work area — start with §1 (Objective) and §3 (Constitution/Doctrine). Everything else follows from those.

§1
Why a Customer-Side Architecture Exists

1.1 The Problem

Before this architecture, the customer system was a collection of features — booking flow, recharge logic, support tools, quality monitoring — each built independently. They worked individually. Together they produced contradictions: the customer app showed one status, the CSP app showed another, and support saw a third. Quality issues triggered false recovery cases. Recharge reminders went out while the service was broken. Nobody owned "what is true about this customer right now."

The root cause was not bad code. It was the absence of governed boundaries — no system defined who owns which truth, who can write to what, and what promises the system makes to the customer.

1.2 How This Differs from CSP-Side Architecture

Wiom's CSP-side architecture (9 locked OSes) governs execution — the CSP is a managed partner who operates within Wiom's rules. CSP-side OSes define what the CSP must do, how they're measured, and what happens when they don't comply.

The customer-side architecture governs truth — the customer is a sovereign person who owes Wiom nothing. Customer-side OSes define what the system knows about the customer, what it observes, what it infers (very little), and what it's constitutionally prohibited from doing with that knowledge.

DimensionCSP-sideCustomer-side
Governed actorManaged partner (CSP)Sovereign person (customer)
Core disciplineExecution governanceTruth governance
Primary riskCSP non-complianceSystem coercion of customer
Anti-corruption focusPreventing gamingPreventing CRM/save/scoring drift
Right to leaveContractually boundedConstitutionally protected

1.3 The Correct Starting Point

The architecture starts from the entity model (who is a customer, what is a service instance), not from features (booking flow, recharge screen). This is deliberate. Features change quarterly. The entity model is the foundation that all features, all OSes, and all future services build on. If the entity model is wrong, every feature built on it inherits the error. If the entity model is right, features can be rebuilt, replaced, or added without architectural regression.

§2
Purpose

This document defines the architecture of Wiom's customer-side system: how the system sees customers, what truth it maintains about them, how different subsystems coordinate, and what constitutional constraints govern every decision.

It is a context note — not a spec. The 8 OS specs are the source of truth for their respective domains. This document governs the structure that holds them together.

One-line architecture: Every OS owns one truth domain, communicates through events, and is constitutionally bound. No OS can overwrite another's truth. The customer's right to leave without friction is architecturally protected.
§3
System Objective

Govern healthy active stock, enable reversible dormancy and re-entry, and ensure true exits are cleanly resolved — without coercive continuation or fake certainty. "Healthy" means:

  • Operational service quality is reliable (SQ Driver)
  • Recovery integrity is strong (Recovery OS)
  • Continuity posture is non-coercive (Continuity OS)
  • Relationship-state is honestly classified (Relationship-State OS)
  • Customer-side truth remains canonical across all surfaces (C1)
Note: More stock alone is not success. Coerced continuation, fake certainty, emotional appeasement, and throughput masquerading as integrity are constitutionally prohibited.
§4
The PayG Paradigm & Value Hierarchy

4.1 PayG Inverts Legacy Telecom

Traditional ISPs assume: the customer commits to a contract, and the service continues unless the customer actively cancels. The system's job is to prevent cancellation.

Wiom inverts this: the customer pays for days, gets days. No contract. No lock-in. No penalty for not recharging. Each recharge is a voluntary continuity reaffirmation — not a renewal of a standing obligation. Absence of recharge is neutral — no deterioration or churn inference occurs from non-recharge alone.

This inversion is the deepest leverage point in the architecture (Meadows LP2 — paradigm). Every OS must respect it. Continuity OS's anti-subscription doctrine, At-Risk Resolution OS's resolution-not-saving principle, and Exit OS's right-to-leave protection all trace back to this paradigm.

Critical: If the architecture ever silently recreates subscription logic — loyalty programs, retention offers, obligation framing, churn-prevention mechanics — it has failed at the paradigm level, regardless of how well individual OSes perform.

4.2 Value Hierarchy and Risk Transfer

At Wiom's customer segment, the power asymmetry is stark:

Wiom holdsCustomer holds
The physical device (netbox)Their choice to recharge or not
The payment (recharge money)Their phone number
The communication channelsTheir time and attention
The service activation switchNothing else

The customer's only power is the right to leave. The architecture must protect this right — not because it's good business strategy (though it is), but because governing the use of asymmetric power is the Constitution's purpose. C2 (system-owned failure) and C4 (clarity of state) exist precisely because Wiom holds the power and the customer holds only their choice.

4.3 Core Service vs Access Model

Service quality (the internet works) is primary. The access model (recharge-based, app-based) is enabling. The access model cannot rescue a broken core service. A customer with reliable internet and a clunky recharge experience is better served than a customer with a beautiful app and an unstable connection. This hierarchy governs investment priority and OS design: SQ Driver and Recovery OS are more fundamental than Continuity OS and At-Risk Resolution OS.

§5
Entity Model

3.1 Two-Level Architecture

The system operates at two entity levels. Every OS except one operates at the lower level.

LevelEntityScopeOwner
Personcustomer_idOne human being, durable across all service instances, exits, re-entries, and servicesCustomer Management OS (the ONLY OS at this level)
Connectionservice_instance_idOne internet connection at one address for one customerAll other OSes

This separation is load-bearing. customer_id persists when service_instance_id is archived. A person who exits and returns gets a new service_instance_id but the same customer_id. CM OS bridges the two levels.

3.2 Entity Lifecycle

EntityCreatedArchivedPersists
customer_idOTP-authenticated sign-up (pre-booking)Never (prunable only if never-booked, per CM-8)Across exits, re-entries, and multi-service expansion
service_instance_idINSTALL_COMPLETED (B2I OS)EXITED_FINAL (Exit OS)Only while service is active or in reservation/exit window

3.3 Household

Household is contextual, not an entity. CM OS records known household only — persons with customer_ids linked via explicit invitation-acceptance. Actual household (everyone using the WiFi) is deliberately Unknown, not a data-quality defect. The system does not infer, estimate, or model household composition beyond known members. (CM-5, CM-6)

3.4 Identity Resolution

Identity resolution is probabilistic with explicit confidence. Two distinct confidence types (resolution and attribution), harm-weighted thresholds, false-split preferred over false-merge. Phone + OTP is strong signal; device fingerprint is medium; advertising ID is weak and never a primary resolution key. (CM-2)

§7
Constitutional Layer

5.1 The Four Constitutional Rules (C1–C4)

Every decision in every OS traces back to these four rules. They cannot be overridden by any individual OS — only by the Constitution Steward.

RuleNameMeaning
C1Canonical TruthWhat the system knows must be the same everywhere. One truth, visible across all surfaces, no contradictions.
C2System-Owned FailureWhen Wiom breaks something, Wiom owns it. The customer never pays for Wiom's mistakes. Attribution honesty is constitutionally required.
C3Restorative RecoveryCompensation must be proportional, rule-based, and automatic — not discretionary. Same rule for every customer. No "save budget."
C4Clarity of StateThe customer must always know what is happening with their service. No opacity, no "we'll get back to you."

5.2 Constitution Steward

The Constitution Steward enforces constitutional compliance across all OSes. Currently: Founder (Satyam). Designated successor: Dhruv. The Steward does not treat customers — the Steward audits departments.

Steward authorities: Doctrine amendment, new-tool approval (D16), extraction-rule gating (CM-12), rate-card review (D8), kill-test invocation (D20), quarterly audit, exception authorization (limited — no D27 cap exceptions per founder ruling).

5.3 Governance Hierarchy

The constitutional layer operates through a three-level hierarchy. This is the most important structural innovation in the architecture — it replaced v1.0's flat "constraint" concept.

LevelWhat it isHow it changesCount
Constitution (C1–C4)Non-negotiable promises to the customerSteward-only, with founder approval4
DoctrinesOS-level constitutional principles that shape OS identitySteward amendment process79 (RS 38 + Exit 26 + CM 15) + 29 (ARR) = 108
InvariantsEnforceable rules with codes and traceable doctrine referencesOS Owner + Steward review110+
ParametersTunable thresholds within invariant boundsOS Owner, no spec amendment needed95 (in SPR v2.0)
You can change a parameter without touching an invariant. Change an invariant without touching a doctrine. Never change a doctrine without the Steward. Never change the Constitution without the Founder.
§8
Signal & Event Layer

6.1 Events as Universal Transport

The customer-side system communicates through events — structured, typed, semantically guaranteed messages emitted by one OS and consumed by others. Events are the architecture's circulatory system.

Every event has: a name, an event class, a payload schema, a partition key, an idempotency key, a semantic guarantee, and a list of consumers with obligation levels.

6.2 Event Classes

ClassMeaningExamples
FACTSomething happened or is trueQUALITY_STATE_CHANGED, EXITED_FINAL, CUSTOMER_CREATED
COMMANDA consumer must execute an actionREFUND_TRIGGERED, RECOVERY_COMMUNICATION_OBLIGATION
REQUESTOne OS requests action from another; recipient owns responseCROSS_OS_FLAG_EMITTED, RESERVATION_EXTENSION_REQUESTED
SYSTEM_ALERTSLA breach or system-level alert requiring escalationCROSS_OS_FLAG_SLA_BREACH

6.3 Consumer Obligation Levels

LevelMeaning
MANDATORYConsumer MUST process. Non-processing is an architectural violation.
EXPECTEDConsumer SHOULD process. Non-processing degrades quality.
INFORMATIONALConsumer MAY consume. Non-processing has no operational impact.

8.2 Key Implication

Technical truth (SQ), recovery truth (Recovery), relationship truth (RS), continuity truth (Continuity), and identity truth (CM) may diverge simultaneously. All can be valid within their domains. A customer can have: Stable quality (SQ), open Recovery Debt (Recovery), Fragile classification (RS), funded continuity (Continuity), and clean identity (CM) — all at the same time. This is not a contradiction. Each OS owns its domain.

§14
Multi-Service Readiness

The architecture is service_instance_id-native. Any customer can enter through any service instance. When Wiom launches a second service (OTT, Wiom Net):

  • New service_instance_ids flow through the same OS stack
  • service_type field on service_instance_id differentiates
  • CM OS provides cross-service person-level context (same customer_id, multiple service_instance_ids)
  • RS OS classifies per service_instance, not per person (no blended cross-service health score — CM-4, INV-CM-20)
  • Each service instance is governed independently by the full OS stack
What must be designed at second-service launch: Expansion OS (cross-sell governance), cross-service obligation handling, and whether household-level wallet pooling is needed (would require CM-5 amendment).

Architecture-Level Falsification (from v1.0, updated)

The architecture is structurally wrong if, after one operating cycle following all 8 OS locks, any TWO of the following hold:

  1. Per-OS governing integrity metrics do not improve materially. The proximate problems are unchanged.
  2. Customer-side state divergence across surfaces persists. C1 has not been operationalized.
  3. A near-term multi-service launch requires material rethinking of the entity model. §5 is wrong.
  4. The PayG paradigm has been silently recreated as subscription logic. Loyalty programs, save offers, or churn-prevention mechanics exist in any OS.
  5. Growing caseload in At-Risk Resolution without declining upstream root causes. The architecture is treating symptoms, not causes.
If two or more hold, the architecture needs redesign, not parameter tuning.

Final Synthesis
Architecture Assessment

The customer-side architecture started as a hypothesis: govern customer truth through bounded OSes operating on shared primitives (Belief, Constraint, Intent) under a four-rule Constitution.

Eight locked OS specs later, the hypothesis was partially validated and partially falsified. What survived: the entity model, the stock model, the Constitution, the bounded-OS discipline, and the event-driven communication. What evolved: Constraints became a four-level governance hierarchy (Constitution → Doctrines → Invariants → Parameters). What was replaced: Belief gave way to confidence-bearing classifications and observables with metadata. What was demoted: Intent became a local concept, not a universal primitive. What emerged that wasn't predicted: the distributed truth network, the anti-corruption architecture, the four OS categories, and the constitutional doctrine system.

The architecture now formally recognizes operational truth (SQ), restorative truth (Recovery), relationship truth (RS), continuity truth (Continuity), identity truth (CM), resolution truth (ARR), and closure truth (Exit) as distinct governed domains. That separation is the core architectural principle protecting the system from collapsing back into telecom-style subscription logic, support-ticket theater, or CRM-driven coercion.

The customer's only power is their choice. The architecture's job is to protect that choice — by governing Wiom's use of asymmetric power honestly, restoratively, and without coercion.
